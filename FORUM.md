>
> Here is what I know about U47 and C12 microphones and our version of these venerable old microphones.
>
> The Advanced Audio CM-47 and CM-12SE tube microphones compare more than favourably to the originals. Most imitators of these microphones copy the tube circuits and tube types exactly. However, in our 40 years of tube circuit experience we have found these circuits can be improved and this is what are goal has been with the AA CM-12, CM-47and CM-67 microphones.
>
> There are several reasons why the Neumann/Telefunken U47 cannot be duplicated exactly and shouldn't be duplicated exactly in our opinion.
>
> The original U47 (pre 1961) used a PVC diaphragm and this M-7 PVC capsule is still made by Gefell but cannot be purchased from Gefell unless you buy it in one of their current microphones. The North American Gefell distributor is an associate of mine and they are located 10 minutes from my current house. We can get you a great deal on a Gefell with an M7 and can take other microphones on trade or even some Ward Beck modules.
>
> The PVC diaphragm's molecular structure reacts differently to audio pressure than the MYLAR capsule used in the K47(M49/M50) and KK67(U67/U87) microphones capsules. PVC capsules tend to dampen the transient response more quickly than they more precise MYLAR capsules. The PVC diaphragm is often referred to as being more "musical" while the MYLAR capsules tend to be more "accurate" and consistent sounding. However, the difference between these two types of diaphragms is very subtle. PVC ages more quickly than MYLAR and gets stiffer over time and in fact tightens up causing the low frequency response to roll-off more quickly below 100hz than the MYLAR as the capsules age. This is the major reason and old original U47 will not sound like a newer one or a re-make with a mylar capsule and why the U47 tends to pop less than the AKG C12 or U67/U47 which has a mylar capsule.
>
> Wunder Audio claims that they use an M7 capsule but in fact they use M7 frames re-skinned with MYLAR and to my old ears the PELUSO CEK-367 capsule will sounds closer to the original M7capsule even though it is also made with a MYLAR diaphragm. John Peluso has done a great job with the CEK-367 to stiffen up the low frequency response and smooth out the typical hf bump in the 7kz to 14khz range in the K47 and KK67 capsules made by Neumann. Wunder Audio also claims that they have copied the same transformer used in the first 200 U47 microphones that were manufactured and that these transformers have a "bigger" low end response. This is not exactly true as the first 200 transformers used in the U47 were power transformers with audio windings and had less high end than later model transformers. The version of the BV8 transformer that we use is flat down to 7hz and has an identical high end response to the transformer used in the later U47 microphones. 
>
> Wunder Audio and the Peluso 2247 SE have a metal UF14 tube which is a 6 volt filament version of the VF14 tube used in the U47 microphones made before 1961. These tubes are always slightly microphonic due to the metal construction of the tube and are not as "hifi" as the later 6072a glass tubes used in the AKG C12 which is my favourite tube. The VF14 was modified for the U47 from a tube used in the audio front end of a German tank transmitter and it design pre-dates the Second World War. The grid to plate capacitance is higher in the VF-14 and UF-14 and this fact coupled with the "Miller effect" which is a major consideration in audio tube circuit design causes the hf response to roll off earlier probably around 25khz depending on the gain of the tube and circuit. Even the UF-14 tubes are very expensive to replace costing at least 20 times more money than the more "hifi" 6072a tube. The 6072a tube was the choice of AKG when the designed the C12 in the late 50's.
>
> If you want a microphone that looks exactly like an original U47 with the darker sound of the original first 200 U47 microphones then you must spend nearly $6000 on the Wunder Audio microphone. However, you can reproduce this sound by using the low pass filter and taking out the response at about 12khz to 14khz with our more "hifi" version. Also, one must remember that the specifications that Neumann supplied with the U47 shows a response between microphones of + and - 4db which is a direct result of the capsule technology of the times. In other words no two brand new U47 microphones would sound exactly the same unless you selected them from a very large stock. The Peluso CEK-367 capsules have a response that are + or -1db from each other.
>
> With our CM-47 we use a selected 6072a tube with a circuit very similar to that of the C12 and U47 capsule preamp. We have optimized the circuit to minimize the "Miller effect" and provide and even lower output impedance than the UF-14 tube. We use the exact same circuit in both our CM-12, CM-47 and CM-67 microphones and these will deliver about 2-3db more headroom that the original microphones. If during the middle of your recording session the 6072a tube fails you can simply go over to your nearest Fender tube guitar amp and pull out a 12AT7 tube put it into our microphone and you are back in business. There is about 2db difference in gain between a 12AT7 and a 6072a tube. With a failed UF-14 tube you must order in a spare tube which can easily cost you $300.
>
> The AKG C-12 uses a C12 MYLAR capsule which is edge fed and this gives it the lower frequency response and the "AIR" in the 15khz range that it is famous for. The Peluso CEK-12 capsule that we use in our CM-12SE duplicates this response within 1db and has an edge fed 6 micron dual capsule. We have also upgraded the output circuit to provide a better damping factor and use the BV11 transformer wound to the same specifications as the transformers in the M49 and M50 microphones and this will produce about 3db more headroom and provide a 3db better signal to noise than the original AKG circuit.
>
> What we have successfully attempted to do is use the inexpensive Chinese bodies with superior Peluso capsules, "state of the art" tube circuits and output transformers to provide an economical and improved version of these venerable old tube microphones. During our years at Ocean Sound in Vancouver we also made improvements to our Studer A80 tape machines, Trident Series 80 consoles and Neve 1073 preamps. We are prone to making circuit improvements rather than copying "flaws" as long as the sound remains musical.
>
> The EQ circuit of the Trident Series 80 and the WBS M462a/M460a-b modules are very similar and have the same "Q" at their lowest settings. The WBS has about 6db more headroom and it is easier to modify the "Q" down to 1.5 which closer emulates the EQ curve of API and NEVE eq's of this era. I did modify our Series 80 midrange "Q" but it is more expensive than modifying a WBS EQ. The WBS EQ is much more flexible that those in the NEVE 1073. The WBS M460/M470 preamp uses the same transformer ratio and secondary input impedance as the Neve 1073/1081 but with a API like Class A/B output circuit which can deliver 6db more headroom internally than the Neve 1073 preamp. The output of a Neve 1073 and WBS M460 or M470 are within a db of each other and were designed to meet the more stringent broadcast standards of the 70's.

## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

## M461M pin in/outs

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Nov 18, 2007|40_

> Hi Guys, the M461 is basically a M460 without the EQ section. I have a manual for one in my shop but will not be there until Sunday. Like all Ward Beck M46xxxx series modules the Pin 22 is chassis, Pin 21 is +25 volts, Pin 20 is common, pin 19 is -25 volts. Pin 18 should be strapped to the common (p20) to have the LED's light. Pin 21 must be strapped to Y and Z to W in order to get the channel on light to come on. I usually put an 82 ohm resistor between Z and W and pin 20 should be strapped to pin X. The resistor drops the 25 volts down to about 23 volts and the incandescent lamp will last much longer.
>
> The balanced inserts pins must be strapped together and the balanced faders pins must be strapped together before any audio will get through to the final output stage. 
>
> Once I find the manual I can give you better wiring notes.

## EQ comparison

_WBS INFO · Feb 28, 2008|41_

> well the difference is mainly it's functionality. Not every EQ has the same flexibility.
>
> M462 family sound the same as they are the same. The circuits are "ALMOST" identical. 
>
> M462 - 3 band full sweeps
> M462A - mid sweep and 2 fixed Q's on lf and hf
> M462B - 4 band full sweeps
>
> All the booster amps in the M462 series are the same, so they way it boosts the signal is identical.
> The M462 are balanced in nd balanced output so they could be patched in or put in line with the M470's insert send.
>
> Ward beck recycled circuits as most companies back then and still do. 
>
> The pre in the M490 is the SAME as the M470 and M480... the EQ in the M480 is derived from the M472
>
> The M472 is closest to the M462A, but with no mid sweep and a fixed high frequency. very simple. it is unbalanced and has no booster amp... the booster comes from the M470's EQ send and return. If you look closely at the M472 there are jumpers internally connected that short the IN and OUT where the M462's would plug in. Any "auxilary" that is balanced such as the M462, the M466 comps, M467 NG. M464 HP LP filters use the same in and out config. so on my console it's fitted all with M462's I could (if i wanted to) swap the EQ with a compressor... kinda cool!
>
> the M472 is simple and it shipped as the stock solution on most L472 consoles.
>
> I will say there are more EQ's like the M472A and the M472C that are stereo, then there are M522, M522A, M522B, M522C etc etc that are loosly based on the M462's.
>
> The EQ in the M460 is like the M462, infact they are the exact same cards. 
>
> In my opinion the M462A are the best, and having a bunch of M462B's have been really nice. 
>
> I will be selling a small rack of (6) M472's in the next week or so; my trip cost me more that I expected.

## 460L and LA phantom power mystery

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jun 24, 2009|42_

> Going from the schematic thats up for the M460A; Here's how I would do it.
>
> Connect 48V from the phantom power source to pin P2-V.
> Connect a 6.8K resistor from P1-B to P1-A
> And
> Connect a 6.8K resistor from P1-B to P1-1
> Last, connect pin P2-18 to P1-B
>
> This will swich 48V on and off, with use of the Solo Switch.
>
> This way, you don't have to mess about inside the module, soldering onto the extra pins of the switches.
>
> I used the same method when I wired a few M470's, and I've had no problems.
>
> As for what happend; all I can guess is that you connected to the wrong part of the switch, and sent the 48V someplace that didn't like it.
> That: or the 460L and LA use the switches differently, and the extra pins you connected too were actually used in those versions.
>
> Using the pins on the connectors is more 'universal,' because all M460's should have been made to plug into any console, and work. (aslong as the console was made to work with 460's!)
> So the parts of the switch that are used may move around from version-to-verson, but the module pin-out is generic.
>
> I'm going from my basic assumption there, someone correct me if im wrong.

> CHAPTER 1
>
> The 8-109 System
>
> The 8-109 series sub-assembly is found in many WBS modules, and acts to provide a actively balanced output at 0, +4, or +8 dBm, with a max output of +30 dBm when powered by the WBS standard +/-25 Vdc power supply. This results in output headrooms of 30, 26, or 22 dB respectively.
>
> As a quick aside to this topic, WBS consoles were unique in the fact that all circuitry outside of the individual modules was actively balanced. A transformer input was only utilized on console inputs. All line level signals between modules, all summing buses, all outputs, and all non-mic jackfield pairs were actively balanced circuits. Console interstage circuitry was generally operated at a nominal 0 dBm level, producing a console internal headroom of 30 dB. Console output circuitry was either +4 (non-broadcast) or +8 dBm (broadcast) dBm with headrooms of 26 or 22 dB respectively.
>
> The 8-109 output cards appeared with the new generation of M460 modules in the mid 1970's. There are many 8-109 cards, tailored for various input applications, subscripts A,B,C,D,E,F,etc, all with an actively balanced +30 peak output. The cards are hybrid, with internal IC circuitry driving transistor output circuitry to provide the actively balanced 1 Watt capability. The output hybrid is totally the creation of Roger K. Beck, who needed only few hours sleep each night, and spent many hours in his R & D labs at WBS and at home.
>
> MRW was Manfred R. Weitzmann, the main technician assisting Roger.

> CHAPTER 2
>
> From M441 to the M460
>
> The M441 input modules and their associated modules represented the original console design by WBS. These early designs were the Phase 1 designs, begun by Ron Ward and Roger Beck as they formed the company in a "garage", upon their departure from McCurdy Radio. Ron Ward was the "wheeler-dealer" and Roger was the "inventor and father of the product", as the company was formed. Virtually all of these early consoles were custom designs to suit the requests of each of the early customers.
>
> In the mid 1970's CBC put out a request-for-quotation for it's soon to be built Vancouver Headquarters on Cambie Street. It required approximately 30 audio consoles for radio, TV, editing, film, and music studios. WBS won this "plum" and it forced the development of the Phase 2 series of consoles beginning with the M460 input modules.
>
> Console design implemented radical updates and improvements. Console metalwork changed from sheet metal to extrusion techniques, with excesses in strenght. End-bells were machined from 1/4 inch aluminum stock. All horizontal elements were custom designed (RKB) extrusions with a captive nut system. The console bodies sat on massive frames welded from rectangular heavy tube stock. Covers and cable ducts abounded, and the console was attractive from any angle.
>
> Module fabrication changed from engraved plastic front panels to an extrusion with with silk-screened metal inset. Mu-metal module covers minimized crosstalk and RF interference. ASSIGN, EQ, L/C, NG, HP/LP and PS modules were all developed, at the same time, to complete these console designs. The very versitile M450 summing, booster, and output module was also created at this time.
>
> The CBC Vancouver project allowed WBS to produce several consoles of the same design for probabaly the first time (radio and edit console designs).

> The M460 series of input modules had numerous subscripts running from M460 to M460M. Because of the application of M460 modules to almost 100% custom consoles, if WBS were able to use an exsisting M460 design, they did. If not, they knocked off a new M460 and gave it the next available subscript letter. M460LA is a coincidence. Another was the M460C - used for consoles provided to CBS-TV.

WBS had a Systems Engineering Dept., which grew to about 10 people, to implement small to moderate customization using existing modular elements. The company reached 110 people at it's peak, in two buildings, at 841 Progress Ave., Scarborough.

> CHAPTER 15
>
> I don't think WBS design people took much from other manufacturers. Rather WBS output was most affected by customer requests and demands. Virtually every early console was a custom design, using exsisting modules where they exsisted, and producing new modules when required. Take the M460 input module as an example - M460, M460A, M460B...thru to... M460M.
>
> As customer budget restraints entered the picture more and more, WBS made a valiant effort to offer "standard" or "stock" designs at considerable savings. The M1203 (table top) and M1204 (full frame) were early stocks using the most sophisticated M460 type modules. The T1202 (table top) developed from the 55 CBC custom small consoles (the banana jacks were a CBC requirement) provided for the 1976 Montreal Olympics. The stock T1202 (more than 100 were made) used new M480 series modules (banana jacks replaced by XLR). For larger stock consoles the L2042 and L3242 units were introduced, now with 470 series modules. The M472 EQ series were about half the cost of the M462 EQ series, because the M472 EQ was not "stand alone", did not have balanced isolated IN and OUT circuitry, but instead caused the equilization to occur interstage within the M470 series input module. Finally there were standard R1200 and R2000 Radio Consoles. The standardization was the frame wiring, and then MIC, LINE, or STEREO input modules could be bought and inserted in any configuration.

## M462 EQ

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Dec 23, 2010|43_

> Let me be the first to say: no amount of electronics will compensate for poor microphone placement and bad acoustics.
>
> The 462s are great. Three bands, top and bottom shelving, mid filter very surgical if needed. 600ohm balanced in and out. No transformers or inductors in the signal path
>
> The 462s are what you want from an EQ, transparent. With all the knobs in the nominal position and the EQ engaged you cannot hear the unit. 
>
> It is the best EQ I've never heard
>
> The 462, 462A, 462B, 562,56A, 562B are all the same EQ, just differently populated PCBs. The EQ design in the the 462 comes from the M460 series of pre-amps. Identical design. (some of the quad 8/Westar/mitsubishi stuff is IDENTICAL to the WBS 462 design) 
>
> I have a feeling people enjoy Neve EQ because when they JAM it all the way they can hear the +/- 18dB of boost / cut. This is the mind over matter thing here: Since you can really hear the EQ working, people think it's doing a good job. The WBS one are +/- 12dB and not inductor based so they are different than Neve. If you ever need to cut and boost 18dB you've got other problems. See above opening statement
>
> The WBS ones use LM301 and LM201 opamps not a 2520 opamp. I don't know why so many people are afraid of packaged chips. It's a bunch of transistors in a can made very small. There is no analog to digital conversion happening in there... don't be afraid of chips.
>
> I would say FUNCTIONALLY the M462 is most similar to an API 550A. BUT the API switches different capacitors in and out to adjust the frequency using a detented switch while the WBS use a smooth infinitely variable resistor to adjust the frequency selection. The WBS eqs can adjust frequencies most neve and most API cannot. 
>
> So, like I said, different.

> Actually the M460 vintage of modules were first plastic escutcheons. As we moved away from the M400 series heavy plastic, then aluminum front assemblies, Mr. Ward (Father Ward) Senior who ran the metal shoppe worked with engineering and developed the later adopted escutcheon and extrusion format that lasted well in to the ST days. The earlier modules were all plastic escutcheons, silk screened, and glued in to place. This was later dropped for aluminum escutcheons that were glued in place, then Erwin Schwalder and Father Ward developed the final phase of the assembly where the escutcheon was screwed in to place with 4 or 5 #440 pan head Phillips screws. The painted escutcheons were painted and backed to make the finish more-or-less permanent. As was the silk screening. I had the first hand experience with these as we were refurbishing 1970's vintage modules in building 1. Margaret League and the ladies would chisel off the old escutcheons, and glue on the new aluminum ones. THen later years they would again chisel off the aluminum ones and replace them with screw down ones. I was involved directly with this process many evenings, weekends and a couple of summers. As for the painting I spent a few years in the stock room, visited the vendors and learned of their various processes.
>
> As for the M666 - that started out as being a unit solely for Jimmy Fong and his crew, and for presenting consoles at the NAB. Yes some were sold to customers, but their initial purpose (as Lydia and I were discussing this very thing at breakfast this AM oddly enough) was so that when a WBS system was on display, the potential buyers saw nothing but WBS equipment. All made by us, designed by Dad, and used by us. The M666 was 1 of 2 true audio amplifyers that we made. I believe TOny - you have teh 20 or 50 watt units??

## Sonic difference between the M441's and the M480's

_WBS INFO · Nov 9, 2012|45_

> Good afternoon Andrew in Montreal!
>
> Phil will assist you with the EQ's for the M460 series and the M402, or I can if you visited Toronto. Long story they are all very different. The first 124 modules had a large input transformer, custom designed by dad of course. That was a card that evolved in to a modular format very quickly. Same innards, different mounting. No sonic difference. They are the most broad in frequency response and resolution. Next are the M600's - of which I use regularly - they are a completely different beast and will not be discussed, but are the next in the chain of evolution. The M460 units of which I use regularly, have a significantly smaller transformer (custom, Dad, etc..), and I feel are the absolute unit for any mixing (they add a little weight to the tone / LF resolution). Tracking they are great as well but I prefer the M600's for that. Next are the M470, M480, M490, M500 series. They all have a smaller transformer 9custom, Dad, etc..). This was designed to meed the changing marketplace - price point. The pre-amplifyer is a little different as well. They all have a sound. They are all exceptional units. They will indeed out-perform British and American Ward Beck (I like ya kid!), but are a flavour. How the low frequencies are cradled is a great place to start the debate. No frequencies are hyped or exaggerated, nor will you measure any ringing on the units - thus no compensating capacitors are really required. I have done several records to tape on my old T1202A (M480) and they are super celan, give you what you give them (like B&K / Schopes microphones), the M460 series gives a little flavour as the low frequencies are snug, but allowed to bloom (think M49), then the M124 cards (real early stuff) well you know how when you wake up in the morning, you cant really see for like 2 - 3 seconds? But at mid day you can read your neighbours e-mails from across the room ..... well these units are the mid-day eyes. Pure transistor softness, no harshness, all frequencies are in-focus, available, and useable. 
>
> The EQ's are very, VERY different. The M402 series are step attenuators, where the later series are all op-amp based. They are musical, easily fit in the mix, solve an issue, bring a guitar out just a little bit, have the 80's snare BARK!!! The M402's if you get the recording or the film ones you are fine, if you get a broadcast one (more common) I advise changing the HF point from 6k to 12k, or put a switch outside the module to change the HF at the flip of a switch. I use mine for gtr and vox most times when EQ is needed. The EQ's found in the M460 modules (and the rest of them for that matter) are all the same concept as each other. They are consistently variable, some are full parametric, some are semi, etc... They are very clean do not add weight to the tone (the module does that nicely!). Using them independently of a trasnformered module you will not experience any tone degradation or addition. they do not really impart a sonic signature. So it will not conflict with your signal, just knob twiddling will!!
>
> As mentioned the M600/M500 units are very diffident. They are amplifyers that are transformer coupled on the input and the output with a mid-stage trim. Hit them hard. You will want many, many more!!
